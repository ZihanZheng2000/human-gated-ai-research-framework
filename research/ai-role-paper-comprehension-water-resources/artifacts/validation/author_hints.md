# Author Hints

Hints were given after the first answer round.

| Question | Hint |
|---|---|
| Q1 | Focus on the Anthropocene: the paper says drought is produced by both natural hydrological processes and human actions, so definitions and analysis need to include both. |
| Q2 | Separate "below normal water availability" from "demand greater than supply." The paper treats impacts and scarcity as related but different from drought itself. |
| Q3 | Think in three categories: climate causes drought, humans cause drought, and humans modify a drought that already exists. |
| Q4 | "Normal" is the threshold. Human activity can change both the actual water state and the baseline used to define what counts as normal. |
| Q5 | The paper shifts from rainfall to hydrological water availability because people depend on rivers, groundwater, reservoirs, and management decisions. |
| Q6 | Look for comparison logic: observed disturbed system vs. meteorological, near-natural, or naturalized reference. Also note metadata, disturbed catchments, and satellites. |
| Q7 | Track inflows, outflows, and storage. Human actions can reduce or increase each of these. |
| Q8 | Physical drought alone is not enough; vulnerability and exposure mediate impacts. |
| Q9 | A feedback is when drought changes human behavior, and that behavior changes the future water system or drought severity. |
| Q10 | Separate conceptual strength from operational difficulty: the framework is holistic but hard to implement because data, attribution, and scale are difficult. |
