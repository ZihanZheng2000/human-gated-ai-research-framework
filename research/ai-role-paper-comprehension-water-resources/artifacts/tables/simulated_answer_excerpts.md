## R1 Undergraduate, Before Hints

- Q1: "The paper is saying drought is not only caused by lack of rain but also
  by people. It wants people to think about drought differently."
- Q2: "Drought, water scarcity, and water shortage all mean there is not enough
  water. I think scarcity is maybe when it lasts longer."
- Q6: "We could look at rainfall and groundwater measurements to see what caused
  the drought."
- Q10: "The framework is good because it includes people, but it may be hard to
  use."

## R1 Undergraduate, After Hints

- Q2: "Drought means water is below normal conditions, while water scarcity is
  when people need more water than the system can supply. A shortage can happen
  when drought and demand problems meet."
- Q6: "The paper suggests comparing observed drought with a climate or natural
  reference, like meteorological drought, an undisturbed catchment, or a
  naturalized model. That helps separate human causes from climate causes."

## R2 Master's Student, Before Hints

- Q3: "Climate-induced drought comes from climate or meteorological anomalies.
  Human-induced drought is caused by water use or land changes. Human-modified
  drought is a natural drought whose severity is changed by human activities."
- Q10: "The framework is useful because it puts human processes into drought
  analysis, but it may be difficult because it needs data on both natural and
  human systems."

## R2 Master's Student, After Hints

- Q5: "A meteorological view is insufficient because people experience and
  manage hydrological water availability, not rainfall alone. Groundwater,
  reservoirs, abstractions, return flows, and management choices can alter the
  drought that propagates through the hydrological system."

## R3 PhD Student, Before Hints

- Q6: "The paper recommends comparing observed hydrological deficits with
  references that isolate climate-induced drought, such as meteorological
  drought, nearby undisturbed catchments, or naturalized model simulations. This
  is difficult because human disturbance metadata and naturalization models are
  often incomplete."

## R3 PhD Student, After Hints

- Q10: "The framework's strength is conceptual integration: it reframes drought
  definitions, driver attribution, impacts, feedbacks, and moving baselines in
  one Anthropocene framework. Its limitation is operationalization: attribution
  requires high-resolution human-water-use data, metadata on disturbed
  catchments, appropriate naturalized baselines, and validation across scales."

## R4 Advisor, Before Hints

- Q10: "The paper's contribution is a conceptual reframing of drought as a
  coupled human-natural phenomenon. It is persuasive as a synthesis and agenda
  paper, but its implementation depends on difficult attribution, sparse
  metadata, scale mismatch, and limited coupled human-hydrological datasets."

## R4 Advisor, After Hints

- Q9: "Feedbacks refer to drought changing human behavior, such as abstraction,
  reservoir operation, land-use choices, or restrictions, which then feed back
  into hydrological storage and future drought severity. They matter because
  policy can either reinforce damaging loops or intentionally alter them to
  mitigate drought and reduce impacts."
