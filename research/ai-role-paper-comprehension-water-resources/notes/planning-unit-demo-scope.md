# Planning Unit: Demo Scope

## User-Confirmed Scope

The first run will be a small demo rather than a publication-scale study.

## Reader Roles

| Role ID | Simulated reader | Intended knowledge profile | Expected behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Undergraduate student | Basic environmental science or water-resources exposure; limited research-paper experience | Can understand abstract-level claims and definitions, struggles with framework logic and methodological implications |
| R2 | Master's student | Some hydrology or water-resources coursework; moderate paper-reading ability | Can follow core argument and some technical distinctions, may miss deeper assumptions or gaps |
| R3 | PhD student | Strong domain training and research-method awareness | Can analyze definitions, framework, methods, limitations, and research gaps |
| R4 | Advisor / senior researcher | Expert-level domain and methodological judgment | Can evaluate contribution, assumptions, novelty, limitations, and future research routes |
| A1 | Omniscient author/evaluator | Gold-standard evaluator grounded in the article text | Creates answer key, hints, and scoring rubric; judges answers against the paper, not against external speculation |

## Candidate Pilot Article

Van Loon, A. F. et al. (2016). "Drought in a human-modified world: reframing drought definitions, understanding, and analysis approaches." Hydrology and Earth System Sciences, 20, 3631-3650. DOI: 10.5194/hess-20-3631-2016.

Rationale:

- Water-resources/hydrology domain.
- Open access with PDF and XML.
- Conceptual and methodological enough to support multi-level comprehension questions.
- The paper itself distinguishes basic terms, frameworks, research gaps, and analysis approaches, which supports a 10-question easy-to-hard ladder.

## Demo Task Design

1. Acquire article text.
2. Author/evaluator generates 10 questions from simple to hard.
3. Four simulated readers answer without hints.
4. Author/evaluator scores answers using a rubric.
5. Author/evaluator gives targeted hints or explanations.
6. Four simulated readers answer again.
7. Compare pre-hint and post-hint scores by role and question difficulty.

## Claim Boundary

The demo can support only this kind of claim:

"In this controlled AI-simulation demo, role prompts with different knowledge profiles produced different levels of apparent article comprehension, and targeted hints changed the quality of their answers."

The demo cannot support this stronger claim:

"Real undergraduate, master's, PhD, and advisor readers would show the same comprehension pattern."
